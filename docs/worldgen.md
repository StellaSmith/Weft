# Worldgen guide

This covers everything that shapes the world: ores, structures, spawns, biomes, and whole dimensions with their own terrain.\
Base builds its overworld with the functions described here, and other mods can change it with the same ones.

## Note
Examples to copy from:
- `mods/testmod/src/lib.rs`: Mars, a planet made only from terrain presets, `void`, an island written by hand, and a biome, a rock needle and boulders added to base's overworld
- `mods/testmod/src/looks.rs`: an obelisk structure standing in another mod's desert
- `mods/base/src/worldgen.rs`: the overworld, with biomes, caves, trees and flowers

## How generation works
The world is made of 16x16x16 cubes, and each one is generated on its own, whenever somebody needs it. That's why a generator has to be a **pure function**: the same seed and position must always give the same blocks, on every machine and in any order. Don't use random numbers, clock, something remembered between cubes for worldgen.

A cube is made in steps: the dimension's terrain first, then its decorations (trees, plants), then ores and structures, and spawns happen later in the finished world. None of these steps cares about the others, which is also what lets a mod add any of them to a dimension that belongs to someone else.

Cube coordinates are block coordinates divided by 16, so cube `y = 4` holds the blocks from 64 to 79.

## Ores
`r.ore(&DIMENSION, &BLOCK)` adds veins of a block to any dimension, yours or another mod's. Another mod's dimension is a handle with its full name, `Dimension::named("base:overworld")`.

```rust
static OVERWORLD: Dimension = Dimension::named("base:overworld");
static TIN_ORE: Block = Block::named("tin_ore");
r.ore(&OVERWORLD, &TIN_ORE).y(-64, 48).veins(4).size(7).replace("#stone_ore_replaceables");
```
- `y(min, max)`: the heights veins can start at
- `veins(n)`: how many veins are started per cube
- `size(n)`: blocks in a vein
- `replace(..)`: what the ore may replace, block names or `#tags` separated by spaces
- `biome("#cave")`: only in biomes with this tag, underground ones included

## Structures
A structure is a fixed shape of blocks the engine places on the surface now and then. You draw it in layers from the bottom up: each layer is a list of rows along z, and each character in a row is one block along x. A key says which block a character means, a space leaves whatever is already there, and `air` clears it.

```rust
r.structure("base:overworld", "obelisk")
    .layer(&["sss", "sss", "sss"])
    .layer(&["   ", " g ", "   "])
    .key('s', "base:sandstone").key('g', "glow_crystal")
    .spacing(128).chance(0.5).biome("#dry").buried(1);
```
That's a sandstone obelisk with a glowing crystal inside, in every dry biome (so deserts) of base's overworld.

Where and how often it appears is set by four calls. `spacing(n)` gives one chance per `n` x `n` blocks and `chance(c)` says how likely that chance is to be taken, while `biome(tag)` limits it to biomes with that tag and `buried(n)` puts the bottom layer that many blocks into the ground. A key can also name a block state, like `furnace[facing=east,lit=true]`.

Structures stand on the surface of the dimension's [terrain](#terrain). A dimension generated by hand reports its surface through `.column(..)` instead, and without it gets no structures. To find one later, `world.structure_near("mymod:obelisk", pos, radius)` gives the closest, even in cubes that don't exist yet.

## Spawns
`r.spawn(entity)` decides where a mob shows up by itself:
- `biome(tag)` and `ground(tag)`: the mob needs a biome with that tag, and a block with that tag under it
- `dimension(name)`: only in that dimension (full name)
- `light(min, max)` and `y(min, max)`: the light level (0 to 15) and heights it needs
- `group(min, max)`: how many appear together
- `weight(n)` and `chance(c)`: how likely it is picked among the rules that fit, and how likely it is to happen per try

```rust
r.spawn(&GHOST).light(0, 4).weight(1);
r.spawn(&SHEEP).biome("#animals").ground("#animal_spawn").light(7, 15).group(2, 3).chance(0.05);
```

## Biomes
A biome says once what it is: the climate it likes, its ground, its colors, plus whatever data and tags you attach.

```rust
static DESERT: Biome = Biome::named("desert");
r.biome(&DESERT).temperature(0.35..).humidity(..0.0).surface("sand", "sand", 3).under("sandstone", 1).tint(&tints::GRASS, 0xbfb755).tag("dry");
```
- `temperature(..)`, `humidity(..)`: where the climate fits it, as ranges like `0.35..` or `..-0.45` (the fields run about -1.4 to 1.4). Without them it fits anywhere.
- `surface(top, filler, depth)`: a top block, `depth` blocks of filler, then the terrain's stone
- `under(block, layers)`: the lowest layers of filler become another block (sandstone under sand)
- `deep_top(block, below)`: a different top block where the ground is lower than `below` (gravel on a sea floor)
- `bare_above(y)`: plain stone above this height
- `frozen(block)`: the water surface turns into this block, and the biome gets the tag `frozen` (base grows no flowers there)
- `tint(&kind, color)`: what blocks tinted with that kind take here, `tints::GRASS`, `FOLIAGE`, `WATER` or a mod's own kind. Kinds you don't give keep their default. To color another mod's biome with your kind, `r.biome_tint(&biome, &kind, color)`.

Tags (`tag("animals")`) are how everything else asks what a biome is, since spawns, ores and structures look at tags and never at names. `set(&COMPONENT, value)` attaches data, like how many trees base grows there.

Registering a biome doesn't put it anywhere. The rules of a dimension's terrain decide where it appears, and that can be underground too: base's `deep_caves` is a biome of whole cubes deep below the surface. Your biome can go into your own dimension or into someone else's, see [changing another mod's terrain](#changing-another-mods-terrain).

## Dimensions
`r.dimension(&MARS)` makes a separate world with its own terrain, sky and gravity, and players get there with `/dim mymod:mars`. It needs a spawn point and something to fill it, usually a [terrain](#terrain) and sometimes a generator written by hand.

```rust
static MARS: Dimension = Dimension::named("mars");
r.dimension(&MARS)
    .spawn(|seed| [8, MARS.column(seed, 8, 8).height + 1, 8])
    .height(-4, 12)
    .sky_above(11)
    .gravity(12.0)
    .fixed_time(5000)
    .sky(0xd8a080, 0xe0b090);
```

All the settings:
- `spawn(f)`: where a new player starts, from the seed. `MARS.column(seed, x, z)` gives the terrain's height and biome anywhere, even where nothing is generated yet
- `player_spawn()`: new players start here and respawn here (without any dimension doing it, the first registered is used)
- `generator(f)`: fills one cube by hand, see [writing a generator](#writing-a-generator)
- `column(f)`: with a generator of your own, the surface height and biome of a column without generating it. Structures stand on this, so it has to agree with your generator
- `height(min_cube, max_cube)`: the limits of the world, in cubes and inclusive
- `sky_above(cube_y)`: cubes at this height and above are empty, so their sky light is free
- `no_sky_light()`: no sky light at all, for caves and the like
- `gravity(g)`: blocks per second squared, 32 by default
- `day_length(ticks)` or `fixed_time(time)`: either a day of that length, or a time of day that never changes
- `sky(sky, fog)`: the two colors
- `ambient(level)`: the lowest light any block gets, 0 to 15
- `no_block_updates()`: nothing grows, falls or flows here

## Terrain
Most dimensions need real terrain, and writing noise by hand is the slow way. `r.terrain(dimension)` builds it from parts: a base height, features that add to it, biome rules that choose a biome and its surface, then water and caves. The engine generates it, so there's no generator to write.

```rust
r.terrain(&MARS)
    .stone("basalt")
    .base_height(60.0)
    .feature(preset::hills(8.0, 220.0))
    .feature(preset::craters(32.0, 16.0, 150.0))
    .feature(preset::dunes(3.0, 22.0).mask(Mask::noise(500.0, 0.15, 0.45)))
    .biome(BiomeRule::new("dust"));
```
`stone` is the block the ground is made of and `base_height` the ground height when nothing is added. Blocks and biomes are given by name, like everywhere else: yours without a prefix, other mods' as `base:sand`.

### Features
A feature adds height to every column. These come ready:

| preset | what it makes |
|---|---|
| `continents(depth)` | land and oceans, `depth` blocks above or below the base |
| `hills(height, size)` | rolling hills |
| `mountains(height, width)` | ranges along ridge lines `width` blocks apart |
| `plateaus(height, size, step)` | flat mesas in terraces |
| `canyons(depth, width)` | winding cuts, filled with water below sea level |
| `dunes(height, wavelength)` | parallel sand waves |
| `craters(radius, depth, spacing)` | bowls with a raised rim |

Your own is `Feature::new(|fields, x, z| height)`. `fields` hands out noise (`fbm`, `ridge` for river-like lines) and the climate (`continent`, `temperature`, `humidity`). The easiest start is copying a preset from `shape` in `crates/noise/src/lib.rs` and changing it. Testmod's needle is one, a column of rock 90 blocks high at one spot of base's overworld.

### Masks
`.mask(..)` limits a feature to some places, with a strength from 0 to 1 that fades in between. `Mask::noise(scale, from, to)` makes patches about `scale` blocks across, and `Mask::Temperature`, `Humidity` and `Continent` take a `(from, to)` range of the climate (swap the two to turn the ramp around). Masks combine with `.and(..)` and flip with `.not()`.

```rust
preset::mountains(150.0, 260.0).mask(Mask::noise(700.0, -0.1, 0.3).salt(0x44).and(Mask::Continent(0.0, 0.2))).named("mountains")
```
`salt` gives a noise its own pattern, so two masks of the same size don't line up, and `named` lets a biome rule ask how strong the feature is (and other mods find it by that name).

### Biome rules
A rule puts a biome into a terrain. It starts from the biome's climate and surface, so `BiomeRule::new("desert")` is a whole rule. Rules are tried in order and the **first one that fits wins**, so specific ones go first and a plain catch-all goes last. What a rule adds is where else it must hold:
- `continent(..)`: continentalness, below 0 is sea and coasts are about `0.0..0.1`
- `height(..)`: the surface height, like `..=SEA + 1`
- `feature("mountains", 0.45..)`: where the named feature is strong

Any of the biome's own settings (`temperature`, `surface`, `frozen`...) can also be given on a rule, and then that rule uses its own.

A rule is named like its biome, which is how other mods point at it (`base:desert`). When one biome has two rules, like base's snowy plains on the coast and inland, the second gets its own name with `.named("snowy_coast")`.

### Water, caves and underground biomes
`.sea(level, "water")` fills everything below a level with water. `.caves(Caves::new("lava", from_y, to_y))` carves tunnels and big caverns, and between those two heights some levels hold flat lava lakes. `.cave_biome("deep_caves", depth, size, threshold)` gives whole cubes at least `depth` blocks down a biome of their own, in patches about `size` across.

### Decorations
Trees, flowers and anything else that stands on the ground go in `.decorate(f)`, which runs for every cube after the terrain filled it. `g.columns()` has the surface `height` and `biome` of each of the cube's columns, indexed `z * 16 + x`. Base does grass, flowers and cacti like this:

```rust
let Some(&cols) = g.columns() else { return };
for z in 0..16usize { for x in 0..16usize {
    let col = &cols[z * 16 + x];
    let y = col.height + 1 - oy;           // the block above the ground, inside this cube
    if (0..16).contains(&y) && g.get(x, y as usize, z) == 0 {
        g.set(x, y as usize, z, TALL_GRASS.id());
    }
}}
```
`g.column(x, z)` gives any other column in world coordinates, and `g.surface_carved(x, z)` says whether a cave opened the ground there, so a tree from a neighboring cube doesn't end up floating over a pit. How much grows where is data on the biome (the components `trees`, `grass`, `tree_kinds` and `flowers`), which is why another mod can give a biome its own trees without touching code, by attaching those components with `r.set_on(Target::Biome("base:forest"), &COMPONENT, value)`.

## Changing another mod's terrain
`r.terrain(&OVERWORLD)` with `OVERWORLD = Dimension::named("base:overworld")` works on someone else's dimension just like on yours. Whatever you call on it is applied after the owner's description, and edits from several mods go in load order, so the result is the same on every machine. Testmod puts a hot, dry biome (`r.biome(&SCORCHED).temperature(0.4..).humidity(..-0.1)...`) into base's deserts like this:

```rust
r.terrain(&OVERWORLD)
    .biome_before("base:desert", BiomeRule::new("scorched"))
    .feature(Feature::new(|_, x, z| if (x - 1000.0).abs().max((z - 1000.0).abs()) < 3.0 { 90.0 } else { 0.0 }).named("needle"))
    .decorate(boulders);
```
Because rules are tried in order, a new biome goes in next to the rule it takes space from: `biome_before` takes the place where both would fit, `biome_after` only what the other leaves. The rest of the edits:

| call | what it does |
|---|---|
| `biome_before(rule, new)`, `biome_after(rule, new)` | adds a rule next to another |
| `replace_biome(rule, new)`, `remove_biome(rule)` | swaps a rule for yours, or takes it out |
| `edit_biome(rule, \|r\| r.temperature(0.5..))` | changes only what the closure sets, the rest of the rule stays |
| `feature(f)`, `remove_feature(name)` | adds a feature or takes a named one out |
| `mask_feature(name, mask)` | limits a named feature further, its mask is multiplied by yours |
| `cave_biome(..)`, `remove_cave_biome(biome)` | the same for underground biomes |
| `decorate(f)` | your own decorations, after the owner's |
| `sea`, `caves`, `stone`, `base_height` | replace the owner's setting, the last mod in load order wins |

An edit whose target isn't there (a typo, or a rule another mod already removed) is skipped with a warning in the log, so a mod made for an older version of base still loads. A dimension generated by hand, like testmod's `void`, has no rules to edit; there a mod can still add ores, structures and spawns, and tag its biomes.

## Writing a generator
When terrain from parts doesn't fit, a dimension can fill its cubes in code with `.generator(f)`. A generator receives a `Gen`, the cube being made. `g.origin()` is the block position of its corner (`g.cube()` is the cube's own), `g.seed()` the world seed, `g.set(x, y, z, state)` and `g.get(x, y, z)` work with blocks (each coordinate 0 to 15, states come from `BLOCK.id()`), and `g.biomes()` is the biome id of each of the 256 columns, indexed `z * 16 + x`.

```rust
fn island(g: &mut Gen) {
    let [ox, oy, oz] = g.origin();
    for y in 0..16usize { for z in 0..16usize { for x in 0..16usize {
        let (wx, wy, wz) = ((ox + x as i32) as f32, oy + y as i32, (oz + z as i32) as f32);
        if (wx * wx + wz * wz).sqrt() < 24.0 && wy <= 63 {
            g.set(x, y, z, STONE.id());
        }
    }}}
}
```
That's `void` from testmod, a round island in an empty dimension.

Two rules to keep, in generators and decorations alike. A cube only writes its own blocks, so for something bigger than a cube (a tree) every cube asks what could reach it and places its own part. Base splits the world into 5x5 cells that each decide from the seed whether they hold a tree, and a cube checks the cells around it (`tree_in_cell` in `mods/base/src/worldgen.rs`). And stay away from `sin`, `cos`, `exp` and the like in terrain, since they give slightly different results on different platforms and neighboring cubes would stop matching. `sqrt` and plain arithmetic are safe, and `noise` (`hash`, `unit`, `perlin2`, `perlin3`, `fbm2`) was written for this.
